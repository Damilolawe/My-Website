01 / Our roots

Two sisters. An entire world of stories.

Gabrielle and Danielle Davenport created BEM for people who love to read, cook, remember and connect.

The beginning

Food touches everything.

Founded as a project by sisters Gabrielle and Danielle Davenport, BEM grew from pop-ups and online bookselling into a permanent Bed-Stuy home at 373 Lewis Avenue in October 2025.

Its premise is expansive: Black culinary culture belongs across history, memoir, recipes, poetry, fiction, children's writing and conversations about identity. BEM celebrates the people and ideas shaping foodways across the African diaspora.

The project was supported by neighbors, independent booksellers, authors, cooks and a successful community crowdfunding effort. The space became a home for readings and culinary events, not just shelves of books.

An unfolding chapter.

2021 / THE IDEA

A new lens on food

Gabrielle and Danielle Davenport establish BEM's mission through books, events and the connection between food and storytelling.

2025 / BED-STUY

A physical home

BEM's Lewis Avenue bookstore and café opens, with a kitchen and gathering space for the community.

2026 / THE PIVOT

Cultural programming

Since August 10, BEM has paused regular in-person bookselling and café service to focus on the BEM Cultural Foundation's programs.

BEM's current schedule and physical access should be confirmed directly; the old bookstore and café hours are no longer current.
