Our story

A second act, served cold.

Salep is the kind of place that only happens when someone decides to chase a craft instead of a career.

The founder

From the Treasury to the counter.

After thirteen years with the U.S. Department of the Treasury, founder Gulden Durdu traded spreadsheets for a scoop and set out to bring authentic Turkish ice cream to New York. Salep is the result — a small, tradition-first shop built for the neighborhood.

The tradition

Centuries in the making.

Turkish ice cream — dondurma — is famous for its stretch and chew. The secret is salep: a fine flour milled from the bulbs of wild orchids, whipped into the mix and pounded until it is dense enough to hang off a spoon. It is a craft passed between generations, and the reason the shop carries the name it does.

The philosophy

Small batches, real ingredients.

Everything is churned in house, in small batches, with real fruit, real nuts and no shortcuts. The American-style flavors sit right next to the Turkish ones, because good ice cream does not need to pick a side.

“A cone should be a little bit of a show.”
